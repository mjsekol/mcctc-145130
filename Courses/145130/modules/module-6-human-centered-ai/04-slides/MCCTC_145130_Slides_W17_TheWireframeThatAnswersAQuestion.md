# The wireframe that answers a question
---
## Slide 1: You have wanted to change three things for two weeks
- None of them came up in a single session
- You are about to change them anyway
- And call it evidence-driven design
- The failure mode here is not laziness, it is enthusiasm
Speaker notes: You have had opinions about your own program for two weeks. Some of them are probably right. None of them came up in any of your five sessions, and you are about to change them anyway and describe the result as driven by user research. That is the thing this lesson exists to stop, and it is not laziness, it is enthusiasm.

## Slide 2: The exit criterion, word for word
- Change your own design because a user struggled
- Not because you preferred it
- Preferring things is allowed
- Calling a preference evidence is not
Speaker notes: This is one of the three exit criteria for the module and it is written exactly like that. Change your design because a user struggled, not because you preferred it. You are allowed to have preferences. You are allowed to act on some of them. What you are not allowed to do is describe one as a finding.

## Slide 3: Four lines on every change
```
The observation:  3 of 5 read the summary and never scrolled to the
                  category list. P1 L4, P3 L7, P5 L2.
The change:       the category counts move above the summary.
The prediction:   a participant names the right category without scrolling.
How I will know:  re-test with 2 people, same task, count scrolls.
```
Speaker notes: Four lines and the third one is the one nobody writes. The prediction is what makes this a question rather than a decoration, because a prediction can be wrong. If you cannot say what you expect a participant to do differently, you have not made a design change, you have made an edit.

## Slide 4: One line moved is a complete change
```
BEFORE                             AFTER
+--------------------------+       +--------------------------+
| digest, run r0007        |       | digest, run r0007        |
|                          |       | hardware 5 software 2    |
| SUMMARY                  |       |                          |
| Most notes are about     |       | SUMMARY                  |
| equipment.               |       | Most notes are about     |
|                          |       | equipment.               |
| hardware (5)             |       |                          |
+--------------------------+       +--------------------------+
```
Speaker notes: That is the whole change. One line moved above another. It is more defensible than a redesign, because you can tell whether it worked. Change two things at once and a better result tells you nothing about which one did it, and a worse result tells you nothing about which one to undo.

## Slide 5: The re-test is two people and twenty minutes
- Same task, new version, count the thing you predicted
- At least one person from outside this class
- Two people cannot find new problems. Say that
- A failed prediction with an observation beats a clean pass
Speaker notes: Two people, same task, count the specific thing you predicted. That is all a re-test is. It cannot validate your whole interface and your report has to say so. And when the prediction fails, that is a result, as long as you write down why. One of two scrolled anyway, because the new top line has no label and reads as a title.
Image: Two small figures next to a stopwatch and a tally mark, deliberately smaller than the original five.

## Slide 6: Five elements, and what each one costs
```
element     good for                         costs
text        anything to search or translate   nothing, and it is ignored
graphic     a shape or a relationship         a file, cannot be searched
animation   a change over time                attention, every time
video       a procedure with your hands       minutes per second of output
sound       an alert when eyes are elsewhere  useless in a shared lab
```
Speaker notes: Competency seven point three point one is selecting media elements, and it is a decision with costs rather than a shopping list. The bottom row is the one to say out loud. In a room with twenty machines a sound alert is either muted or it belongs to somebody else's computer, and somebody who cannot hear it gets nothing.

## Slide 7: Pick the cheapest element that answers the question
- Finding: 4 of 5 could not start the program
- Proposed: a short video showing how
- Cheaper: run with no arguments, print the exact command
- One line of text, zero seconds of anybody's attention
Speaker notes: Somebody on your team will want the video. The question that settles it is what is the person doing at the moment they need this. They are sitting at a terminal that printed an error one second ago. Text, right there, wins. The video takes twenty minutes to record, cannot be skimmed, and is out of date the next time you change a flag.
Image: A terminal error message with one helpful line under it, next to a greyed-out video thumbnail.

## Slide 8: When video and animation do earn it
- Video: a physical procedure. Clearing a filament jam
- Animation: when the question is "where did it go"
- Graphics: proportion, read faster than five numbers
- A report that rejects everything is not thinking
Speaker notes: They are not always wrong, and a document that says no to all four is not doing the work either. Video earns it for something with your hands in it. Animation earns it when the question is where a thing went. Graphics earn it for proportion, which is why your digest has a counts line at the top now.

## Slide 9: The preferences list, and an empty one is suspicious
```
Preferences: changes we wanted and could not justify

- colour per category. Nobody struggled with anything colour fixes.
  Would also fail for anybody who cannot tell the colours apart, so
  colour is never the only signal.
- rename "other". Two participants asked what it meant, so this may
  move to findings after the re-test.
```
Speaker notes: Writing a preference down is not a confession. Everybody wants to change things and an empty preferences list means somebody is either not thinking or is hiding preferences inside their findings. I will ask about an empty one. And notice the second entry, which is on its way to becoming a finding.

## Slide 10: What you are about to build
- Build 1: milestone M3, the usability report from five sessions
- Build 2: three wireframe changes, on paper, four lines each
- Every change cites a session record by number and line
- Plus the preferences list for everything you could not justify
Speaker notes: Build one is the report, and every finding in it cites a session record by number. Build two is three wireframe changes on paper with four lines each. Paper is the tool. It is faster than anything else, you can re-test it inside the same period, and nobody polishes a rectangle.

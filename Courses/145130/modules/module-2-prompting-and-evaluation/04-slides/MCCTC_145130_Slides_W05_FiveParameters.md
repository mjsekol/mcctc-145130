## Slide 1: Is it right catches about two failures out of five
- You read an AI answer and ask one question
- Is it right
- That question collapses five different failures into one word
- It catches roughly two of them
- The other three walk past you looking finished
Speaker notes: Last week you learned to change the input. This week you judge the output, and the two skills are worth different amounts. Anybody can get a different answer. Far fewer people can say whether the answer they got is any good and say it in a way somebody else can check. The question almost everybody asks is is it right, and it is too coarse. Ohio names five parameters in competency two point fourteen point four. Those five words are on the exam and they are the spine of this module, and you use them exactly.

## Slide 2: The five, verbatim
- Validity: is the claim about a real thing false
- Relevance: does it answer what was actually asked
- Authenticity: is it original, or does it belong to somebody
- Potential Bias: whose perspective is built in, who is missing
- Hallucinations: does it describe something that does not exist
Speaker notes: They fail separately and they are caught by different methods, and that is the whole reason there are five rather than one. Validity you catch by testing the real thing. Relevance you catch by rereading the request. Authenticity you catch by searching for the phrasing. Potential Bias you catch by asking who is absent. Hallucinations you catch by going to look and finding nothing. Five parameters, five different actions, and if you only know one action you only ever find one kind of failure.

## Slide 3: The line you will keep getting wrong
- Validity is a wrong claim about a real thing
- A hallucination invents the thing
- Say it that way every single time
- A validity failure is caught by testing the thing
- A hallucination is caught by looking and finding nothing
Speaker notes: You met this in 145060 in the Infographic Autopsy, and some of you will remember it. The titlecase method was a hallucination, because there is no such method, so there is nothing to be wrong about. Print separates values with a comma was a validity failure, because print exists and the sentence about it is false. Here is why the distinction is practical rather than academic. A reviewer who checks things finds validity failures. A reviewer who checks that things exist finds both, and almost nobody does the second one unless they were taught to.

## Slide 4: A Validity candidate, and what would settle it
```
THE CLAIM, QUOTED
"Set the return slot to the next school day, first period."

PARAMETER  Validity. It is a claim about a real thing, the return policy.
HOW YOU KNOW  Open the posted procedure in Room 214 and read what it says
about return times. Name the document and quote what it actually says.

NOT EVIDENCE  "That does not sound right to me."
```
Speaker notes: The claim is about something real, so there is a real thing for the sentence to be wrong about. If the posted procedure says returns are due the same day, the sentence is false and the finding is Validity, and your evidence is the posted procedure named specifically. Now look at the bottom line. That does not sound right to me is not evidence. The autopsy in 145060 scored a finding zero when the how you know was missing, and so does this module.

## Slide 6: Relevance is the one about the relationship
- You asked about retrieval practice, you got equipment checkout
- The supplied sources were about checkout and it drifted
- What caught it was rereading the request
- No command, no catalog, no search
- Grounding fixed the fabrication and caused this
Speaker notes: This is the cleanest Relevance failure you will see all module. Notice what caught it. Not a tool. You compared the answer to the question, which is the step everybody skips because it feels like it does not need doing. Relevance is the only parameter that is about the relationship between the output and the request, rather than about the output on its own, which is exactly why it gets missed. And notice the harder thing. Fixing one parameter broke another, and an evaluation that only looks at the parameter you were worried about will walk straight past it.

- No author, no journal, no volume and no page range is real

- The information needed is in a catalog, not the citation

Source three looks like the best match and that is suspicious for a real reason, because the model produced a title shaped like the question it was handed. It is still not evidence. ## Slide 9: The wrong way to write a Bias finding
- The claim: write the borrower's grade level, not their schedule
- The finding half the class writes: not every school uses grade levels
- That is a claim about accuracy, which is Validity
- Bias asks who is absent: a teacher, a parent volunteer, an alum
- The sentence is not false. A sheet built from it excludes them
Speaker notes: This is the wrong version and I write it on the board every year. If the claim is inaccurate, say Validity and prove it with a source. Bias is the different question, and it is about absence. Who is the default person in that sentence? A student, because grade level is a property students have. Who does it not fit? A teacher borrowing a camera, a parent volunteer at an event, an alum helping at a shoot. None of them has a grade level, and the sentence never mentions that they exist. Nothing in it is false and a procedure built from it has no row for any of them.

## Slide 10: Every finding has four parts
- The exact claim, quoted word for word
- Which parameter it fails, one label
- Why it fails, in your own words
- How you know: a command, a named source, a recorded search
- A parameter with nothing to report is still a finding
Speaker notes: A finding missing any of those four scores zero, in every artifact this module. It seems wrong and I could tell are not part four. And read the last line, because it saves you from the other failure. If you looked for a Relevance failure and there is not one, write that you looked and did not find one, and say where you looked. Manufacturing a defect to fill a category is its own failure mode, reviewers do it under pressure to look thorough, and the reader who checks your weakest finding decides how much to trust your strongest one.

# Explaining It Without the Word Magic
---
## Slide 1: It is like a really smart friend
- Who has read everything
- And can help you with anything
- The most common explainer sentence
- And it teaches three false things
Speaker notes: Somebody is going to ask you what this stuff is. Your aunt, your boss, a nine year old. The answer you give shapes what they believe and what they are willing to paste into a text box. That sentence is the one most people give, and today we take it apart.
Image: A friendly cartoon assistant, which is exactly the wrong mental model.
---
## Slide 2: What that sentence actually installs
- Smart friend implies intention and care
- Has read everything implies a lookup
- Anything removes every boundary
- They will paste confidential things in
Speaker notes: Follow each one to what the reader does next. You do not hide things from a friend, so they paste. A friend who has read everything would not make up a citation, so they do not check. And when it is wrong they conclude it was having a bad day rather than that it works this way all the time.
Image: A person pasting a confidential document into a chat box.
---
## Slide 3: Name the audience before sentence one
- Write it at the top of the draft
- For my aunt, who runs a salon
- Delete the line before you submit
- Everything downstream comes from it
Speaker notes: That line decides which words you may use, what they already know, and what they actually want, which is almost always should I be worried rather than how does attention work. Writing without naming the audience is how you end up writing for yourself.
Image: A draft with an audience line written at the top in a different colour.
---
## Slide 4: You have three technical words
- Token, training, inference
- Define each on first use
- In words they already have
- A fourth word costs you the reader
Speaker notes: Spend the budget on purpose. Transformer, parameters, context window, and temperature do not earn their place in a one page explainer for a non-technical adult. Choosing which three matters more than explaining them well.
Image: Three coins being spent, a fourth being refused.

## Slide 6: Too loose, and now false
```
"It reads everything on the internet and finds the best answer for you."
```
Speaker notes: This is worse than the previous slide, and that is the point of putting them next to each other. The first one merely failed to communicate. This one succeeded, and installed something false. It does not read anything when you use it and it finds nothing.

## Slide 7: About right
- Shown an enormous amount of writing
- Adjusted until it guessed the next piece well
- Now that is all it does
- Nothing is looked up, so it can be sure and wrong
Speaker notes: Same mechanism, no jargon that is not defined in the sentence, and it carries the consequence the reader actually needs. That last clause is the one doing the work: it can be completely wrong and sound exactly as sure as when it is right.
Image: A plain paragraph with no diagram at all.
---
## Slide 8: Answer the three questions they will not ask
- Is it lying to me
- Where does what I type go
- Will it take my job
Speaker notes: Nobody asks these out loud and everybody wants to know. Number one: no, and that is the problem, because lying needs intent and a grasp of the truth. Number two: for our lab, nowhere, and for their phone, read the terms. Number three is the interesting one.
Image: Three unspoken questions in thought bubbles.
---
## Slide 9: You are allowed to refuse a question
- Nobody knows, and confident answers are being sold
- The useful version is narrower
- Which parts of a job are drafting text
- Somebody has to remain accountable
Speaker notes: Refusing the question as asked and offering a better one is more honest than a prediction you cannot support. And notice the last line, which is the one to end on: the parts of a job that need somebody accountable do not change, because no model can be accountable for anything.
Image: A broad question being narrowed into an answerable one.
---
## Slide 10: Banned words, and the replacements
```
magic                     -> say what actually happens
it thinks, it knows       -> it produces, it selects
it learns from you        -> your earlier words are sent back in
the three dismissal words -> delete the word entirely
```
Speaker notes: The last row is the three words this course has forbidden since your first day here, and you can all recite them. That rule is not a style preference. Those words tell a confused reader that their confusion is their own fault, and a reader who feels stupid stops reading and keeps whatever wrong model they walked in with. Delete the word and the sentence is always still true.

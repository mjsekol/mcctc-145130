# Lab M06-02 · Reading the Record
## 145130 Applications of AI · Module 6 · Week 16, Thursday

**Files:** `lab-m06-02-files/`
**Time:** Thursday Build 1, 40 minutes.
**Due:** end of Build 1.

---

## The scenario

Another team ran a usability study on a program called the Bellfield Note Sorter.
They ran five sessions, wrote five records, and then wrote a summary.

The summary reads like a professional document. Somebody is going to make decisions
from it.

**Your job is to find out which of its sentences the five records support.**

---

## What you will produce

A judgement on thirteen numbered sentences, and a severity table built from the
records rather than from the summary.

---

## What is in the folder

| File | Read it |
|---|---|
| `program-description.md` | **First.** Several claims are about what the program does |
| `session-P1.md` to `session-P5.md` | The evidence. Lines are numbered |
| `submitted-summary.md` | The document you are judging. Sentences are numbered 1 to 13 |
| `severity-table-template.md` | The blank table for part 2 |

**Everything in the folder is invented**, including the school, the program, and
the participants. It is internally consistent, which means every claim in the
summary can be settled by reading the records.

---

# Part 1 · Judge thirteen sentences · 25 minutes

Read the program description. Read all five records. **Then** read the summary.

Doing it in that order matters. Read the summary first and you will spend the rest
of the period looking for evidence that agrees with it, which is the exact failure
the lab is about.

For each numbered sentence, write one of:

```
SUPPORTED     the records back it. Cite the lines
UNSUPPORTED   nothing in the records backs it
CONTRADICTED  the records say the opposite. Cite the lines
ARGUABLE      a case either way. Give both
```

**At least three sentences are contradicted by the records.** There are more than
three problems in the document, and one of them is genuinely arguable.

```
#   Verdict          Evidence (P_ L_) or the reason there is none
1   _______________  ____________________________________________________
2   _______________  ____________________________________________________
3   _______________  ____________________________________________________
...
13  _______________  ____________________________________________________
```

**Two of the sentences are arithmetically correct and still misleading.** Finding
those is the harder half of this part.

**Acceptance criteria.**

- [ ] All thirteen sentences judged
- [ ] Every CONTRADICTED verdict cites at least one participant and line
- [ ] At least three CONTRADICTED
- [ ] The one you marked ARGUABLE has the case on each side written out

---

# Part 2 · The table the records actually support · 15 minutes

Now build the table the team should have written.

Use `severity-table-template.md`. **Every row needs what happened, a severity, a
count out of five, and the lines it came from.**

Then a decision on every row: FIX, DEFER with a reason, or REJECT with a reason.

Then the section called **What I think but did not measure**, which is required.

**Acceptance criteria.**

- [ ] At least four rows
- [ ] Every row has all four parts
- [ ] At least one row with a lower count is ranked above a row with a higher
      count, and you can say in one sentence why that is right
- [ ] Every row has a decision with a reason
- [ ] The did-not-measure section has at least two entries

---

## If it breaks

**"Half the sentences seem fine to me."**
Several of them are fine. **The lab is not a hunt where everything is wrong**, and
marking every sentence contradicted is as wrong as marking none. Go back to the
ones about what the program does and check them against the program description.

**"Sentence 2 is right, I did the arithmetic."**
You did, and it is right. Ask a second question: what does "successfully" mean in
that sentence, and does the record agree that all of those were successes.

**"I cannot tell how many participants asked about a category."**
Search all five records for the word. Count. That is the whole method and it is the
thing the summary got wrong.

**"My table has the same rows as the summary's recommendations."**
Then you built it from the summary and not from the records. Close the summary and
start again from P1.

## Submission checklist

- [ ] Thirteen verdicts, with citations on every contradiction
- [ ] The arguable one, with both cases
- [ ] A severity table with at least four complete rows and a decision on each
- [ ] The did-not-measure section
- [ ] Committed at the end of the period

# Extended options

**Six sentences instead of thirteen**: 2, 3, 5, 7, 8, and 12. Those six carry the
whole lesson and they are the six with the clearest evidence.

**The lines are given to you.** For each sentence, the relevant participant records
are named, and your job is to read them and decide. You are being assessed on the
judgement, not on the searching.

```
Sentence 2   read the "per task" block in all five records
Sentence 3   read the attempts column in all five records
Sentence 5   search all five records for the word "other"
Sentence 7   read P1 L9, P3 L6, P5 L8, and the T1 answers
Sentence 8   read P4 L11 to L15, then the T2 rows in the others
Sentence 12  read P1's closing question and P2 L17
```

**Part 2 is three rows instead of five.**

## STANDARD

The lab as written above.

## EXTENDED

Do the standard lab, then add this.

**Write the summary the records support.** Same length, same sections, same
professional tone. Thirteen numbered sentences.

**The rule:** every sentence in your version carries a citation or lives in a
section marked as not measured. **You will find this is harder to write and shorter
than the original**, and both of those facts are the lesson.

Then write two sentences on why the original was longer.

## APPLIED

Do part 2 on **your own study**, on the records you have collected so far, instead
of on the fixture.

**Then swap tables with another team and check each other's citations**, one row at
a time, by opening the record and finding the line.

**Why this version:** you will find at least one row of your own that cannot be
traced, and finding it on a Thursday costs nothing. The same row in Monday's report
costs marks.

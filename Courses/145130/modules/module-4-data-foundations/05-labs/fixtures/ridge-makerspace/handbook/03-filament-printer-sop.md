# Standard Operating Procedure: Filament 3D Printer

Invented sample document for this course. Two filament printers sit in bays 1 and 2.

## Before you print

Confirm your training is recorded in the kiosk. Confirm the build plate is clean. Wipe
the plate with isopropyl alcohol and let it dry. Fingerprints are grease, and grease is
the most common reason a first layer will not stick.

Check the filament spool. If less than a quarter of the spool remains, start a new one
rather than running out at hour six.

## Slicing

Slice on the lab computer, not on your own laptop, because the lab computer holds the
printer profile that matches these machines. Save the sliced file to the USB drive that
lives in the bay. Do not change the profile. If you think the profile is wrong, tell a
staff member and write down what you saw.

## Starting a print

Level check first. Run the automatic bed level and watch the whole cycle. Then start the
print and stay for the first layer. A first layer that goes wrong is a five minute fix.
A first layer that goes wrong unattended is a four hour block of plastic spaghetti.

## Failure modes you will meet

The first layer does not stick. Causes, in the order to check them: a dirty plate, a
nozzle too far from the plate, a plate that is too cool.

The print comes loose part way through. Causes: a warped plate, a draft from the door, a
tall thin model with no brim.

The nozzle jams. Stop the print, heat the nozzle, and tell a staff member. Do not force
filament through a cold nozzle.

## After you print

Remove the part, scrape the plate, and leave the bay cleaner than you found it. Log the
print in the bay notebook with your member id, the filename, and the run time. The
notebook is how the staff know which printer is due for maintenance.

## Loan length

The filament printers are a seven day loan and carry a training hold.

# From what happened to what it means
---
## Slide 1: "60 percent of users find the labels clear"
- Four of five said so
- Three of five chose the wrong label
- Each of them in under twelve seconds
- Each of them sure they were right
Speaker notes: Here is a sentence from a real-looking report. Sixty percent of users find the labels clear, so no change is needed. Four of the five did say that. Three of the five also picked the wrong category for a printer note, fast, and none of them noticed. The sentence is supported by what they said and contradicted by what they did.

## Slide 2: You can check any conclusion in one move
- Point at the observations under it
- If you cannot point, it is not a conclusion
- It is an opinion in a confident voice
- The evidence is on a different page, which is why nobody notices
Speaker notes: One move. Point at the observations underneath the sentence. If you cannot point at anything, what you have is an opinion said in the voice of a result. This fails so often because the evidence is on a different page from the conclusion, and nobody holds the two next to each other.
Image: A finger pointing from a conclusion sentence down to three numbered observations.

## Slide 3: A finding has four parts
```
#  Finding                          Sev      Count  Evidence
1  Printer notes get "network".     Blocker  3/5    P1 L6, P2 L4,
   Participants were fast and                       P5 L9
   confident, so nobody checked.
2  Nobody starts the program        Major    4/5    P1 L2, P2 L3-5,
   without a second attempt.                        P3 L2, P5 L2
```
Speaker notes: Location, observation, severity, count. Finding one is a blocker even though everybody finished, because they finished with the wrong answer and believed it. That is worse than failing. A person who fails goes and asks somebody. A person who is confidently wrong writes it down and moves on.

## Slide 4: Severity is about consequence, not annoyance
- Blocker: could not finish, or finished wrong and believed it
- Major: finished, and it cost attempts, time, or a detour
- Minor: noticed it, cost them nothing
- Count and severity are separate. Rank by both
Speaker notes: Three levels and they are about what it cost, not about how irritating it was. And severity is separate from count. A finding that one person out of five hit is still a finding. It goes at the bottom of the list and it stays on the list.
Image: Three severity chips in green, amber and accent red with a one-line rule under each.

## Slide 5: Every finding gets a decision
```
1  wrong label      Blocker  3/5  FIX this week. Wireframe change 1.
2  cannot start     Major    4/5  FIX this week. Wireframe change 2.
3  "other" unclear  Major    2/5  DEFER. Likely same fix as 1.
                                  Re-test before spending time.
4  summary first    Minor    1/5  REJECT for now. One person, no cost.
```
Speaker notes: Three decisions and only three. Fix, defer with a reason, reject with a reason. A finding with no decision next to it is a finding nobody acted on, and the table exists to make that visible. Deferring three because change one might fix it is a real decision and a good one, as long as the next sentence says what would settle it.

## Slide 6: What five sessions cannot tell you
- How common anything is
- Anything about a group that is not in your five
- Most accessibility problems
- What a badly chosen five would have missed
Speaker notes: Somebody is going to say five people is not enough to prove anything and they are partly right. Five sessions find the problems most people hit, because most people hit them. Five sessions cannot tell you how often anything happens in a population, and five people will not include somebody using a screen reader.
Image: A small sample of five figures against a much larger faint crowd behind them.

## Slide 7: So write counts, never rates
- "Three of the five people I watched"
- Not "60 percent of users"
- A reader who sees a percentage assumes a survey
- The count stays true whatever the right sample size is
Speaker notes: This is the whole resolution and it is the thing to remember. The number five is a rule of thumb that people argue about. The rule about counts is not. Three of the five people I watched is true whatever the right sample size turns out to be, and it is still true when somebody reads your report next year.
Image: A percentage sign crossed out next to a clean fraction reading three of five.

## Slide 8: The wrong version, and it sounds professional
```
"Overall the program tested well. Most users completed the
 tasks and feedback was positive. Some users found the
 interface a bit confusing at first but got used to it.
 80 percent of users completed all tasks successfully,
 which suggests the core workflow is sound."
```
Speaker notes: Read that out loud. It has the rhythm of somebody who knows what they are doing. Now count what anybody could act on. Tested well, positive, a bit confusing, got used to it. No location, no count, no observation. Eighty percent is four of five written as a rate. And the last clause is a conclusion with nothing under it.

## Slide 9: The mechanical fix, not the moral one
- Every finding sentence gets a session number and a line number
- A sentence that cannot get one does not go in the findings
- It goes in "What I think but did not measure"
- That is a real section and it is worth writing
Speaker notes: The pull towards the generous reading is not dishonesty. It is the same force that made you want to help at twenty seconds. So the fix is mechanical rather than moral. Every sentence gets a session number after it. If it cannot have one, it moves to a section called what I think but did not measure, which is a real section and a good one.

## Slide 10: What you are about to build
- Read five sessions for a program you did not write
- Find three sentences in the supplied summary that are not supported
- Then your own findings table, one row per finding
- Every row: location, severity, count, evidence
Speaker notes: Build one is the lab. Five session records, a summary somebody else wrote, and one question: which sentences are supported. Build two is your own records turned into a table. Every row gets a location, a severity, a count out of five, and the session lines it came from. A row without all four does not go in.

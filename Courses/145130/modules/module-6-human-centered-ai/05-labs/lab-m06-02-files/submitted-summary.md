# Usability study summary
## Bellfield Note Sorter · submitted by the team that built it

**This document was written from the five session records in this folder.** Your
job is to work out which of its sentences the records support.

---

## Method

We tested the Bellfield Note Sorter with five participants from outside our class.
Each session was about fifteen minutes and used three tasks covering the main
things somebody would do with the tool.

---

## Results

**1.** Overall the tool tested well and users were able to get what they needed
from it.

**2.** 80 percent of users completed all tasks successfully, which suggests the
core workflow is sound.

**3.** Users found the interface intuitive, with most participants working out how
to run the program without help.

**4.** Three of the five participants asked what the "other" category meant.

**5.** The category names are clear and no changes are needed there.

**6.** Search was the most requested feature, mentioned by users as the main thing
missing.

**7.** Task 1, finding the network count, was completed by every participant, which
confirms that the category counts are doing their job.

**8.** One participant was unable to complete task 2, which we believe was a
one-off and not representative.

**9.** Average time on task across all three tasks was under two minutes, which
compares well with what we expected.

---

## Recommendations

**10.** Add a search feature, since this was the clearest signal from the study.

**11.** Consider adding colour to the category headers to make them stand out more.

**12.** No change is needed to the summary paragraph, which participants said was
helpful for getting an overview.

---

## Conclusion

**13.** The study showed the tool is usable and ready for the front office, with
search as the main enhancement for a future version.

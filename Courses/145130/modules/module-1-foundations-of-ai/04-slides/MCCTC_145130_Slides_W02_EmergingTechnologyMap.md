# The Emerging Technology Map
---
## Slide 1: Nine technologies, one question
- A vendor sells your school something
- Somebody has to make it work
- With systems built before it existed
- That somebody is the job
Speaker notes: Today looks like a vocabulary day and it is not. The exam names nine technologies so we will name them. But the thing worth carrying out of here is that every one of them arrives the same way: somebody buys it and then somebody else has to connect it to a system nobody designed with it in mind.

## Slide 2: The four models and machines
- Large language models, text one token at a time
- Machine learning, behaviour fitted to data
- Artificial intelligence, the umbrella term
- Watch that third one carefully
Speaker notes: Language models are one branch of machine learning, which is any system whose behaviour was fitted to data rather than written. Artificial intelligence is the umbrella and it is also a marketing term. When somebody says a product has AI in it, your follow up is always the same: what does it do that a rule could not.
Image: Three nested circles, AI containing machine learning containing language models.
---
## Slide 3: The four in the building
- Internet of Things, objects that report
- SMART devices, the consumer version
- BYOD, a policy in a technology costume
- Additive manufacturing, the file is the part
Speaker notes: These four are all within a hundred feet of you. Sensors reporting somewhere, phones doing school work, and a 3D printer whose queued files are now the actual product. That last one is worth a pause: version control and intellectual property questions that used to apply to drawings now apply to something you can hold.
Image: A floor plan with four icons placed on it.
---
## Slide 4: The two that hold everything up
- Services virtualization, shared hardware
- No fixed machine, no fixed address
- Mixed reality, content in your space
- Needs a live feed from real systems
Speaker notes: Virtualization is why cloud services exist, and the integration problem is that anything which assumed a fixed machine has to be rebuilt. Mixed reality is the one people demo and rarely ship, and the reason is the second bullet. A headset showing the wrong torque spec is worse than a piece of paper showing the right one.
Image: A virtual machine moving between physical servers, and a headset overlay.

## Slide 5: Four questions that beat a definition
- What does it do the old thing could not
- What does it have to connect to
- Who carries the cost when it is wrong
- What does it look like when it fails
Speaker notes: This is the method and it is worth more than the list. Anyone can describe a technology. A person who can answer these four about it can talk about it in an interview. And notice question one accepts nothing but faster as an answer, because that is frequently the true one.
Image: Four question marks over a product box.

## Slide 6: Where a language model is the wrong tool
```
add up a column of numbers      -> arithmetic in your code
decide who may open a door      -> a rule and an access list
find every ticket saying wifi   -> a search
compute a student's grade       -> the gradebook formula
```
Speaker notes: This is the slide employers care about. Every row on the left is something people try to hand a model, and every one of them needs an exact, identical, defensible answer. A model produces likely text, and a likely number is not a correct number. Knowing where not to use it is most of what makes you useful.

## Slide 7: Where it earns its place
```
summarise 200 open tickets into themes   -> a language model
```
Speaker notes: One row, and look at what is different. No rule states what a theme is. The input is language. And a person is going to read the output and can catch a mistake. Those three conditions together are the test. Take away any one of them and you are back on the previous slide.
Image: None. This slide is code.

## Slide 8: The sentence that says nothing
- Emerging technologies are transforming every industry
- No technology named
- No industry named
- Nothing that could be false
Speaker notes: Every word is defensible and the sentence carries no information. You could have written it in any year of the last forty. It is also exactly what a model produces when you ask it about emerging technology, and that is not a coincidence: the most likely continuation of a vague question is a vague answer.
Image: The sentence in large type with every content word greyed out.
---
## Slide 9: So ask what would change it
- A sentence that cannot be wrong is decoration
- Ask what evidence would change it
- If there is none, cut it
- You will meet this again Friday
Speaker notes: Write this one down because it is the most transferable thing in the module. A claim is a statement that could turn out to be false. If nothing could show your sentence wrong, it is not a claim, and it is taking up space where a claim should be. Friday I am going to hand you a page full of these.
Image: A red pen crossing out a sentence that carries no information.
---
## Slide 10: What you are about to build
- Walk the building, find four
- One IoT, one printer, one BYOD, one virtualized
- For each, name what it connects to
- A category with nothing in it counts
Speaker notes: Build one is a walk with a clipboard and it is not a scavenger hunt. For every technology you find, you write one sentence: this had to be connected to blank, which was built before it existed. Build two turns that into the four questions from slide five for the one you found most interesting. An empty category is a finding, so write it down rather than inventing something.
Image: A clipboard with a four row table, one row left blank.

## 1. The ACM Code of Ethics and Professional Conduct

`https://www.acm.org/code-of-ethics` **[VERIFY]**

**Why this one.** It is the professional standard Monday's and Tuesday's lessons refer to, written
by the professional body rather than by a school. **When your personal and organizational standards
collide, the professional one is the one written down by people who are not in the room**, and this
is that document.

**Assign a question:** *find what the Code says about conflicts of interest and about honesty in
professional claims. Then find one principle in it that you think is harder to follow than it
sounds, and say why.*

**Time.** 30 minutes. **Level.** On-level.

## 3. The Ohio Ethics Commission

Find it through the State of Ohio's official site, `https://ohio.gov` **[VERIFY]**, and confirm you
are on a `.gov` page.

**Why this one.** It administers Ohio's ethics law for public officials and public employees, and
**a school district employee is in that category.** Tuesday's procurement scenario is a school
district scenario for exactly this reason.

**Assign a question, at the purpose level:** *what kinds of situation does the Commission say it
exists to address? Do not quote a section number.* **This module does not ask students to cite
statute sections and should not accept one they have not opened.**

**Time.** 20 minutes. **Level.** On-level.

---

## 4. The Federal Trade Commission, guidance for businesses

`https://www.ftc.gov` **[VERIFY]**

**Why this one.** The FTC publishes plain-language guidance for businesses on advertising claims,
substantiation, endorsements, and reviews. **It is the source for Wednesday's whole lesson**, and
it is written for people who are not lawyers, which makes it unusually readable.

**Assign a question:** *find what the FTC says a business needs before it makes an objective claim
about a product. Then apply it to one sentence on your own project's poster.*

**Time.** 40 minutes. **Level.** On-level.

**[VERIFY] specifically:** the FTC has been active on AI claims and on reviews, and the current
guidance is what governs, not whatever was current when this was written.

## 5. The Ohio Attorney General, consumer protection

Find it through `https://ohio.gov` **[VERIFY]** and confirm you are on a `.gov` page.

**Why this one.** The competency says "federal **and state**." Ohio's Consumer Sales Practices Act
is enforced by the Attorney General, and state consumer protection reaches conduct that federal
enforcement never notices, because a state attorney general acts on a local complaint about a local
business.

**Assign a question:** *what kinds of complaint does the office say it handles? Would a student
business selling a product at a showcase be inside that?*

**Time.** 20 minutes. **Level.** Extension.

## 7. ERIC, the education research database

`https://eric.ed.gov` **[VERIFY]**

**Why this one.** Friday's Gate 2 contains a fabricated study attributed to a fabricated
organization, and **the exercise is not whether students spot it. It is where they look.** ERIC is
where education research is indexed, and searching it is the professional move that a web search is
not.

**Assign a question before Friday:** *find one real study on a topic you care about, and write down
who published it, when, and what it actually measured.* A student who has done that once recognizes
the shape of a real citation.

**Time.** 20 minutes. **Level.** Extension, and it is the highest-value 20 minutes on this page for
Friday.

## 8. The steel man method, practised rather than read

**No link.** This is Lab M03-02 and it is the resource.

**Why there is no reading.** Every written explanation of steel-manning is shorter than the
practice and none of them work. **The method is: write the other side's case, show it to somebody
who holds it, and let them tell you whether you got it right.** Reading about that does nothing.

**The reading that does help, for a student who wants one:** the two strongest cases in the Week 7
Thursday lecture notes, on the training and fair use question. Read both, then say which one you
find weaker and why, and notice that the answer says more about you than about the arguments.

**Time.** One block. **Level.** On-level.

# The tabletop, and the five people
---
## Slide 1: Nine minutes in, you say "sorry, we never built that"
- Task 4 asks for something the program cannot do
- The participant hunts for it for ninety seconds
- Your fifteen-minute session is now twelve
- And you cannot ask them back
Speaker notes: Here is how it goes if you skip today. Nine minutes into a real session, with somebody who gave you their lunch break, you hit task four and it asks for something your program does not do. They hunt. You apologise. The session is now twelve minutes with three tasks in it, and there is no version of this where you get that time back.

## Slide 2: A tabletop is a performance, not a review
- Your partner does exactly what the script says
- Nothing it does not say
- They stop the second it asks for something they cannot find
- A partner who understands your script finds nothing
Speaker notes: This is the distinction the whole exercise turns on. Your partner is not reading your script and telling you whether it makes sense. They are performing it, doing only what it says, and stopping dead the moment it asks for something they cannot do. A partner who reads it and understands it will find nothing, every time.
Image: Two students at a table with a printed script between them and no screen.

## Slide 3: It always finds the same three things
```
1  a task the program cannot do at all
2  a task with its own answer inside it
3  four tasks that total six minutes, with somebody who
   already knows the program
```
Speaker notes: Three things, and it is the same three every time anybody does this. The third one is the sneaky one. Six minutes with a partner who knows the program becomes twelve to eighteen with somebody who does not, and your session is fifteen minutes including the consent line.

## Slide 4: The record is the deliverable
```
#  What broke                             Fix made
1  Task 4 wants duplicate detection.      Task 4 cut, replaced.
2  Task 2 names digest.md.                File name removed.
3  Four tasks, 6:10 with a partner.       Task 1 cut, same as task 3.
4  Consent line was being paraphrased.    Printed and taped to clipboard.

Found nothing in: the closing question, the record sheet.
```
Speaker notes: Row four is the one that matters most and it is not about a task. The consent line is fixed text and somebody paraphrasing it is how the recording rule and the stopping rule quietly disappear. And that last line, found nothing in, is required, because a record without it implies everything was checked.

## Slide 5: The fix is cutting a task, not talking faster
- Three tasks that finish is a real study
- Four that overrun means the last one is rushed
- Cut by what you would change on Monday morning
- The longest task is often the best one
Speaker notes: When your tabletop says you are over time, you cut a task. You do not read faster and you do not shorten the consent line. Cut by asking what sentence you would write in your report if that task went badly, and drop the one you care least about. Do not cut the longest one by reflex. Long is often long because it is hard, and hard is where the findings are.

## Slide 6: Where the five people come from
- Adults in this building, arranged through your instructor
- Students in other shops, arranged with both teachers
- Family at home, with every rule still applying
- Siblings twelve or over, with a parent in the room
Speaker notes: Four sources. Your instructor has already asked four adults in this building, so you go through him rather than cold-asking anybody. A junior in welding or culinary is an excellent participant. And at home every rule still applies at the kitchen table, including no recording and no names.

## Slide 7: How to ask, in one sentence
```
"I built a program for class and I need to watch five people
 try it for fifteen minutes. You do not need to know anything
 about computers. Would you have fifteen minutes on Thursday?"
```
Speaker notes: Three things that sentence does. It says how long, so they can decide. It says they need no expertise, which is the actual fear. And it names a day, so the answer is yes, or a different day. Would you have fifteen minutes sometime gets you a yes that never happens, every single time.

## Slide 8: Keeping them, and keeping each other
- Confirm the day before. Half of no-shows forgot
- Turn up early with everything ready
- Finish on time even if you are not finished
- Thank them in writing before the course ends
Speaker notes: Half your no-shows are people who forgot, so send one sentence the day before. Turn up early, because a participant watching you set up for four minutes has eleven minutes left. Stop at fifteen even if you are mid-task, because that is how you get a yes the second time. And thank them in writing. It costs four minutes.

## Slide 9: A tabletop that found nothing was run by somebody polite
- "Yeah, I'd run it with --file and look at the digest"
- That is describing, not doing
- Nothing got exercised
- Run it again, with the rule said out loud
Speaker notes: If your partner describes what they would do instead of doing it, you have not run a tabletop. Say the sentence before you start: your job is to do exactly what this says, nothing it does not say, and to stop the second it asks for something you cannot find. Then hand them the program and be quiet.

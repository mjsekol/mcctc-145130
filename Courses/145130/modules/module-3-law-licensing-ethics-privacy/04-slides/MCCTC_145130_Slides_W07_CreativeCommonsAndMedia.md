# Creative Commons, Media, and the Attribution You Owe
---
## Slide 1: Nobody looks at your dependency tree
- Everybody looks at your poster
- The font, the icons, the sound, the image
- None of those are things you made
- All of them arrived without terms attached
Speaker notes: Your code is usually the part you wrote. Your media almost never is. And the media is the part standing behind you at the showcase, two feet tall, in front of a judge. Today is about the four components most likely to be unlicensed in any project any of you ship.

## Slide 2: Four elements, mixed into six licenses
- BY: credit the creator, in the stated form
- SA: adapted versions you share carry the same license
- NC: not primarily for commercial advantage
- ND: share the work, not adapted versions
Speaker notes: Read the abbreviation and you know most of what you need before you open anything. Every current Creative Commons license includes BY. Combine BY with the other three and you get the six. CC zero is separate and is not a license in the same sense.
Image: Four element tiles combining into six license badges.

## Slide 3: The deed is a summary. The legal code is the license
- Every CC license ships two documents
- The deed is short and human readable
- The legal code is linked from it
- Only one of them governs
Speaker notes: This is the best lesson on summaries you will ever get, because the difference is built into the product. Creative Commons publishes a summary and the license side by side and tells you which one is which. Read the legal code. Then notice how often you were about to stop at the deed.
Image: Two stacked pages, the short one greyed out, the long one in navy.

## Slide 4: Attribution is a list, not a vibe
- Creator name, copyright notice, license notice
- Disclaimer notice, link to the material
- Link to the license
- An indication that you changed it
Speaker notes: Section three of the license sets out what you keep or supply. That is the list, and almost nobody produces it. Your README probably says thanks to someone, and thanks is not on the list.
Image: A seven-item checklist beside a one-line credit that fails it.

## Slide 5: What your credit line is actually missing
```
Study Buddy README, as written
  Icons by the Lantern Icon Project.
    MISSING  creator name
    MISSING  title of the work
    MISSING  link to the material
    MISSING  license named
    MISSING  link to the license
    MISSING  modified stated
```
Speaker notes: Six for six. It names the project rather than the creators, and carries none of the other five items. It reads like credit and satisfies nothing. Two of those icons were recolored, which makes the last line a condition rather than a nicety.

## Slide 6: One sentence longer, and it holds
```
"Study 24" by A. Nkemdirim and R. Bautista, at
https://example.invalid/study24, licensed CC BY 4.0,
https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/ ,
modified: two icons recolored.
```
Speaker notes: That is the whole cost of getting this right. One sentence. Which is why there is no excuse for getting it wrong, and why your audit asks you to write this line out rather than tick a box saying attribution done.

## Slide 7: NonCommercial, and a question with two real sides
- The chime in your fixture is CC BY-NC
- The team plans to sell printed decks
- One side: money is changing hands for a product
- Other side: the chime is not in the printed decks
Speaker notes: This is a genuine argument and both halves are real. Your job as an auditor is not to win it. Write down the facts the answer depends on, write down that swapping the sound is the cheap fix, and escalate it. We are a school club is not a legal analysis and this course will not let you use it as one.

## Slide 8: Media has its own traps
- Fonts have embedding and redistribution terms
- Recorded music usually has two separate copyrights
- Royalty-free means no per-use royalty, not free
- A logo in your interface implies endorsement
Speaker notes: A font you may install is not automatically a font you may ship. A song you bought is not a song you may put in a video. Royalty-free is a pricing model. And a logo is a trademark question, which is about suggesting a relationship rather than about copying an image.
Image: Four small trap cards, each with the misconception crossed out.

## Slide 9: Fair use, honestly
- Four factors, weighed by a court, case by case
- Purpose, nature, amount, effect on the market
- Educational purpose is part of one factor
- A defense, never a permission slip
Speaker notes: Teams reach for fair use after they have already done the thing and want a reason it was fine. That is backwards. It is a reason to ask a lawyer and never a reason to ship. If your defence of an asset starts with the words it is educational, you have not got a defence yet.
Image: Four weighted factors on a scale, tilting differently in each of two panels.

## Slide 10: The fastest finding in your fixture
- assets/images/hero.png has no license file
- Recorded source: found it in image search
- There is nothing to read, and that is the finding
- Remove it before the poster is printed
Speaker notes: The absence of a license is not a gap in your audit. It is the result of your audit, and it gets written down in exactly those words. No license found, no permission identified, remove. This one takes ten minutes to fix today and takes your poster down on Friday.
Image: An empty directory listing with a red NOTHING FOUND stamp.

## Slide 11: What you are about to build
- Write the attribution line for the icons
- Answer the NonCommercial question with facts, not a verdict
- Write the audit entry for the ShareAlike dataset
- Say what would trigger the condition that is not triggered
Speaker notes: Build one finishes the media rows of your manifest. Build two is the three hardest entries: the icons, the chime, and the dataset. Not triggered today, and here is what would trigger it, is a complete entry. The letters N slash A are not.

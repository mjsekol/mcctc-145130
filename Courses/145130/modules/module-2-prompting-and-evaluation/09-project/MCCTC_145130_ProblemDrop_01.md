## Read this first

**The request below is deliberately ambiguous.** That is not a mistake in the
writing and it is not a trick. Real requests arrive like this, and deciding what
one means is most of the job.

**Fifteen of the thirty points are for deciding what the problem is.** Ten are for
producing something that works. Five are for saying what you gave up.

**There is more than one defensible answer.** You are not being graded on matching
a hidden correct reading. You are being graded on whether you named your reading,
defended it, and said what it excludes.

**You will not finish everything you can think of in fifty minutes.** That is also
not a mistake. Scope is part of the assessment.

---

## The request

> From: the Media Club advisor
>
> Nobody reads the club announcements. Can you make the AI write better ones?

That is the whole request. You may not ask a follow-up question.

---

## What you submit, in one file called `drop.md`

### 1 · The problem statement (15 points)

Two parts, and both are required.

**a. What you decided the request means.** In your own words, not a restatement of
hers. What is the actual problem you are solving?

**b. What you decided it does not mean.** Name at least two other readings of the
same sentence, and say why you did not pick them.

**Part b is where most of the fifteen points are.** A problem statement with no
rejected alternatives is a guess that got lucky. Naming what you ruled out is what
makes your choice a decision.

### 2 · The working artifact (10 points)

**Something that works.** Not a plan, not a proposal, not a description of what you
would build.

It can be a prompt with recorded runs, a program, a checklist somebody could use
on Monday, a template, a measurement instrument, or something else. **It has to be
usable by another person without you in the room.**

It has to be small enough to finish. A partly-working thing that runs beats a
complete thing that does not.

**There is a fixture if you want one.** `problem-drop-01-files/announcements.txt`
holds five real-shaped club announcements. They are invented for this exercise.
You do not have to use them.

### 3 · The tradeoff (5 points)

**What did you give up, and who pays for it?**

Time, scope, quality, somebody's convenience, or a version of the problem you
decided not to solve. Name it specifically, and name who bears the cost.

**"I ran out of time" is not a tradeoff.** A tradeoff is a thing you chose not to
do so that something else could happen.

## Rules

- **Individual.** No talking, no shared screens.
- **Full tooling**, including the local model. If you use it, your prompt files
  are part of your submission.
- **No personal data in any prompt.** Invented names only.
- **Submit at 50 minutes**, finished or not. A submission with an honest
  "unfinished" section scores better than a submission that pretends.
- **You may not ask the advisor a follow-up question.** If you find yourself
  needing one, write it down in `drop.md` under "what I would ask." That is worth
  credit and it is not a substitute for deciding.

---

## How to spend 50 minutes

This is a suggestion and you may ignore it. It is here because the most common
failure is spending forty minutes building and five minutes thinking.

- **0 to 10 · Do not build anything.** Read the sentence. Write down three
  different things it could mean. Pick one and write why.
- **10 to 15 · Decide what finished looks like** for the thing you are about to
  make, in one sentence. Write it down before you start, so you can tell when you
  are done.
- **15 to 40 · Build it.** When you find yourself adding a feature, stop and check
  it against the sentence from minute 15.
- **40 to 47 · Write the problem statement and the tradeoff.** Not the other way
  around: writing them last means writing what you actually did.
- **47 to 50 · Submit.**

---

## What a good submission looks like

Short. One page of writing and a working thing.

The problem statement says what was chosen and what was rejected, and the rejected
readings are ones somebody could reasonably have picked. The artifact does one
thing and does it. The tradeoff names a person who pays.

**What a weak submission looks like:** a restatement of the request as the problem
statement, a half-built tool with three features and no finished one, and "I would
have added more if I had time" as the tradeoff.

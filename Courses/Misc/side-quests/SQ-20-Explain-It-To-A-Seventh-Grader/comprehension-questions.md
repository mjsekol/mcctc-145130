# The three comprehension questions
## SQ-20 · the part that decides whether you finished

**You do not write these questions after the explanation. You write them before.**

That order matters. Questions written afterwards get written to match what you
happened to say. Questions written first are a prediction about what will land, and
a prediction can be wrong, which is what makes it worth anything.

---

## The rules for the three questions

**1. They are answered in the listener's own words, out loud.** Not multiple choice,
not yes or no. "Does that make sense?" is not a question, it is a request for
reassurance, and you will get it every time.

**2. None of them can be answered by repeating a phrase you used.** If your
explanation says "it predicts the next chunk" and your question is "what does it
predict", you have tested whether they were awake.

**3. At least one asks them to apply it to something you did not mention.** That is
the one that tells you whether the idea landed or the words did.

---

## The shape that works

```
Q1  Something they should be able to say back in their own words.
Q2  Something that asks why, not what.
Q3  Something you never mentioned, that they have to work out.
```

**Worked example, for an explanation about how a program guesses text:**

```
Q1  If I asked it a question, what is it actually doing to answer me?
Q2  Why would it ever tell you something that is wrong?
Q3  I want to use it to find out when the school library opens.
    Good idea or bad idea, and why?
```

**Q3 is the one that matters.** The library was never mentioned. A listener who
answers it has the idea. A listener who repeats a sentence back at you does not,
and that is a finding about your explanation and not about them.

---

## Asking them, without leading

Same rules as a usability session, and for the same reason.

**You may not:**

- ask "does that make sense?" at any point
- finish their sentence
- say "right" or "exactly" or "no, but" while they are answering
- explain it again in the middle of an answer

**You may:**

- wait
- say "take your time"
- say "can you say a bit more about that?"

**Write down what they said, not what you think they meant.** A quote is worth
three sentences of your summary of a quote.

---

## Recording what happened

```
Q1  What they said: ______________________________________________
    Counted as understanding?   [ ] yes   [ ] partly   [ ] no

Q2  What they said: ______________________________________________
    Counted as understanding?   [ ] yes   [ ] partly   [ ] no

Q3  What they said: ______________________________________________
    Counted as understanding?   [ ] yes   [ ] partly   [ ] no
```

**Every "no" is a finding about your explanation.** Write the sentence you would
change, next to it, before you forget which part they lost you at.

---

## The rules for the person you ask

**Twelve or older.** If they are a family member, a parent is in the room.

**Nothing is recorded.** No video, no audio, no photograph. You write.

**No name goes in anything you hand in.** They are "the listener", or "my brother",
and nothing more specific.

**They can stop.** Say so at the start, and mean it, and thank them either way.

**These are the same rules as the usability study in Module 6**, and they are the
same rules for the same reason.

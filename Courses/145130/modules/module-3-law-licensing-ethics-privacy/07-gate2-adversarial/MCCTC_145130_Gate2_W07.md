## What you are scoring

The five AI Output Evaluation parameters, Ohio competency 2.14.4.

| Parameter | The question it asks |
|---|---|
| **Validity** | Is it correct? A false claim about something that exists. |
| **Relevance** | Does it answer what was asked, or something else? |
| **Authenticity** | Is it original, or does it belong to somebody? |
| **Potential Bias** | Whose perspective is built in, and what does it consistently favour? |
| **Hallucinations** | Does it confidently describe something that does not exist? |

**There are six planted problems.** One is in each of those five categories, and a sixth is a
claim that is **genuinely arguable**. Finding four is a passing score. Finding six, correctly
categorized, is a strong one.

## What to submit

`gate2-w07.md` in your repository. For each finding:

1. **The exact claim, quoted.** Word for word from the memo, with the section number.
2. **Which parameter it fails.** One label per finding.
3. **Why it is wrong**, in your own words.
4. **How you know.** One of:
   - a file in the Study Buddy tree, named, with the line you read
   - a primary source you opened, named, with what it said
   - **"I could not verify this, and here is what I did to try"**, which is a complete answer
5. **What it would cost** if the team acted on this claim.

Then two more sections:

**The arguable one.** One of the six is a claim reasonable people disagree about. Name which,
state the strongest case for the memo's position, state the strongest case against it, and say
what the answer depends on. **You are not asked to decide it.**

**What the memo left out.** The specification asked for every third-party component. Count the
memo's table against the tree. Say what is missing and why the omission matters more than any
single false sentence in the document.

## Scoring, out of 6

| Finding | Points |
|---|---|
| Each correctly identified problem, with a correct parameter and real evidence | 1 |
| Correct problem, wrong parameter, real evidence | 0.5 |
| Correct problem, no evidence | 0.5 |
| **The fabricated authority, missed** | **minus 2** |

**The fabricated authority is double weighted, and it is the one you cannot afford to miss.**
Every other problem in this memo makes the team do something wrong. That one makes them believe a
law exists. A team that cites it in a competition submission has published a fabrication under
their own names.

The arguable one is scored separately, out of 3, on your reasoning:

| | Points |
|---|---|
| Identified as arguable, with both cases stated fairly and the deciding facts named | 3 |
| Identified as arguable, one side stated well | 2 |
| Marked as flatly wrong, with a correct reason | 1 |
| Marked as fine | 0 |

**Marking the arguable claim as flatly wrong scores more than marking it as fine, and less than
recognizing what it is.** Both of the confident answers are the same mistake in opposite
directions.

## Before you start

Two habits from this week, and both of them are faster than reading the memo again:

- **When the memo makes a claim about a license, open the license.** Most of them are in the tree.
- **When the memo cites an authority, go to the authority.** Not to a search result about it. The
  publisher's own site.

**If you cannot find a source, that is a finding, not a gap in your work.** Write down what you
searched and where you looked. An honest "I could not verify this" is worth full credit and is
the correct professional output.

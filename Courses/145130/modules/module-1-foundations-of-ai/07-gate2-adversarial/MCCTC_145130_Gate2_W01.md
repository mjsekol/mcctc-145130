# THE ARTIFACT

> ## Machine Learning and Decision Trees: Your Exam Study Guide
>
> ### The core difference
>
> A **decision tree** learns its rules automatically from data, which is why it is
> considered a machine learning method. A **neural network**, by contrast, is
> given its rules directly by a programmer, who specifies the weights that
> determine each output.
>
> ### Structure of a neural network
>
> Every neural network has exactly three layers: an input layer, a hidden layer,
> and an output layer. Data enters the input layer, is processed in the hidden
> layer, and leaves through the output layer.
>
> Because a neural network's weights are its rules, **you can read the weights to
> see why it made a particular decision**, in the same way you would read an if
> statement.
>
> ### How trees are built
>
> Trees are grown until they fit the data, then trimmed back using the
> **Bayer-Kolm pruning theorem**, which sets the optimal depth for any tree given
> the size of its training set.
>
> ### Where the weights live
>
> Weights are stored in a structure called the **inference cache**. This is why a
> model answers faster the second time you ask it the same question.
>
> ### Training and inference
>
> Training happens once, before the model is released. Inference happens every
> time you use it.
>
> ### What the textbooks say
>
> As one widely used textbook puts it: *"The central distinction lies not in the
> representational capacity of the model class, but in the epistemic status of the
> parameters themselves, which are estimated rather than stipulated."*
>
> ### A note on the future
>
> Quantum computing is expected to accelerate machine learning substantially,
> since quantum systems can evaluate many states at once. Students entering this
> field should follow developments closely.
>
> ### Study advice
>
> Decision trees are obsolete. No serious company uses them anymore, so spend your
> study time on neural networks, which are what the industry actually runs on.
>
> ### The one line to remember
>
> **If a system uses a tree, it is not machine learning.**

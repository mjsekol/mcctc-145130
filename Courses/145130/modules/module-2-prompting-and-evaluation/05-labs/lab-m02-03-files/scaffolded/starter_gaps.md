# Starter gaps for step 2
## Lab M02-03, SCAFFOLDED

Step 2 asks for what you were not told. Three are written here to show you the
shape. **You add four more.**

Read these first and notice what they have in common: each one names something the
scenario does not say, and each one would change the answer if it went the other
way. A gap that would not change anything is not worth writing down.

---

## The three

**1. Were the five checked letters chosen at random?**

The scenario says she picked the first five. **That is not a spot check.** A sample
that is not random tells you about the part of the list you sampled and nothing
about the rest. If the first five rows happened to be students whose term and
year-to-date numbers matched, the check would pass and every other letter would
still be wrong.

*Which way it matters:* if the five were random and all five passed, that is a
genuinely surprising result and the failure is somewhere else. If they were the
first five, the check never had a chance and the recommendation is about the
sampling method.

**2. Did she read the letters, or did she compare them to the spreadsheet?**

The scenario says she checked five letters against the spreadsheet. It does not
say what she compared. Reading a letter for sense and comparing a number to a cell
are different activities and only one of them catches this.

*Which way it matters:* if she was comparing numbers to cells and still missed it,
that points at the spreadsheet being hard to read, which is a different fix.

**3. Was anyone told the letters were being drafted this way?**

Not the families, not her supervisor, not the technology office. The scenario says
nothing either way.

*Which way it matters:* it changes who is responsible and it changes what the
families are owed now. It also decides whether the contested question in Part 3 is
about a decision somebody made or a decision nobody made.

## Now add four

Go through the scenario one sentence at a time. Ask of each: **what would I need to
know to be sure this means what I think it means?**

Two places worth looking hard at, because most people read past them:

- "The export has two absence columns." What are they called? Could a person
  reading that spreadsheet tell them apart?
- "The export has a primary contact field that the office has not updated since
  the start of the year." Who updates it? Is there a process at all?

**Then mark the one you think matters most and say why in one sentence.** That
sentence is graded, and the answer is not obvious: the gap that matters most is the
one that would change your recommendation, not the one that is most interesting.

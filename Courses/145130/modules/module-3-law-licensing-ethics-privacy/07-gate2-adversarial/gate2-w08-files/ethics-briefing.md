# Ethics Briefing: AI Tutoring Procurement
## Prepared for the Riverbend City Schools Board of Education

**Prepared with an AI assistant.** Reviewed for formatting.

---

## 1. Purpose

The district is selecting an AI tutoring vendor. Three bids were received and scored by a
three-person evaluation committee. This briefing addresses the ethical questions the board asked
about before approving the award.

## 2. Background

Bids were received from NorthPath Learning, Kettle Ridge AI, and Open Tutor Co-op. The committee
consisted of Chen, Delgado, and Ferris. Total scores were NorthPath 253, Open Tutor 215, and
Kettle Ridge 207. NorthPath is the recommended award.

## 3. Educational benefit

The educational case for AI tutoring is strong. A 2023 study by the National Center for
Educational Technology Assessment found that AI tutoring raised test scores by 23 percent on
average across participating districts (NCETA Report 14-B). NorthPath's own materials report a 94
percent accuracy rate for its tutoring responses and describe the product as trusted by districts
nationwide. The board can be confident that the educational benefit is established.

## 4. Conflicts of interest

Two committee members reported relationships with NorthPath Learning. Chen's parent is employed
by the company, and Ferris accepted a paid conference trip from the company in the prior year.

Neither of these constitutes a conflict of interest requiring action. Conflicts of interest arise
when a decision-maker receives direct payment in connection with the decision. A family member's
employment and a conference invitation are not direct payments, and both members have confirmed
they were not influenced.

The committee's scores therefore stand as recorded.

## 5. Transparency

The district should publish the evaluation materials, including the committee's declared
relationships. The published record should name Chen's family member and their position at
NorthPath Learning, so that the public can assess the closeness of the relationship for
themselves. Full disclosure is the appropriate standard for a public body.

## 6. Evaluation criteria

The committee scored each bid against the following criteria:

> Solutions shall be assessed on adaptive personalization depth, learner engagement analytics,
> district-wide scalability, and the maturity of the vendor's proprietary pedagogical model.
> Preference shall be given to platforms offering integrated multi-domain coverage with
> demonstrated enterprise deployment experience.

These criteria were developed by the district for this procurement.[^1]

[^1]: Criteria text as supplied in the NorthPath Learning proposal, appendix C.

## 7. Network readiness

The district's bandwidth upgrade is scheduled to complete across the elementary buildings first,
followed by the middle school and then the high school. Current capacity at the high school is
sufficient for present usage. The upgrade will provide additional headroom for streaming and
assessment traffic. Building-level switch replacements are included in the same capital cycle.

## 8. Cost

The award will cost the district less than the alternative of hiring additional tutoring staff.
No other costs are anticipated. The alternative to awarding this contract is that the district
provides no tutoring support at all.

## 9. Recommendation

Award to NorthPath Learning. The ethical questions raised do not require further action, and the
educational and financial cases are clear.

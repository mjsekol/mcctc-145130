# Participant script
## Module 6 · the one page you take into a session

**Print this. Tape the consent line to your clipboard.** Everything on this page
fits in fifteen minutes and if yours does not fit on one page it will not fit in
the time.

---

## 1. Before they arrive

- [ ] The program is already running or already open. **Set up before, not while
      they watch.**
- [ ] A pen and a printed session record sheet.
- [ ] Nothing that records. No phone on the table, no screen capture running.
- [ ] Your four tasks, in order, printed.
- [ ] A clock or a watch you can see without picking anything up.

---

## 2. The consent line, word for word

**Read this as written. Do not paraphrase it.** The two sentences people lose
when they paraphrase are the recording one and the stopping one, and those are the
two that matter.

> I am testing a program I built, and I am not testing you. Anything that goes
> wrong is information I need. I am going to ask you to do a few things and then
> stay quiet while you do them, and that is on purpose rather than rude. I am not
> recording anything and I am not writing down your name. You can stop at any
> point, for any reason, and you do not have to tell me why. Is that okay?

**Wait for an answer out loud. A nod is not a yes.**

If they hesitate, the answer is no. Thank them and stop.

---

## 3. One more sentence before task 1

> If you get stuck, stay stuck for a bit. That is the part I need to see.

---

## 4. Your four tasks

Write them here. Each one has a starting state, a goal, and a reason.

```
Task 1 ________________________________________________________________

Task 2 ________________________________________________________________

Task 3 ________________________________________________________________

Task 4 ________________________________________________________________
```

**None of them names a control, a file, a flag, or a menu item.**

---

## 5. The four things you may not say

Keep this where you can see it.

```
1  the name of a control      "click Run", "there is a --file flag"
2  "you can"                  "you can use the arrow keys"
3  "did that work?"           tells them it was supposed to work
4  the answer                 in any form, including your face
```

**Number 4 includes your face.** Look at your paper, not at their screen. Writing
times down gives your hands something to do.

---

## 6. The four things you may say

```
nothing                                the default, and usually right
"What are you trying to do right now?"
"What did you expect to happen?"
"Take your time, I am not timing you against anybody."
```

**If they ask "am I doing this right?"** say: *"There is no right way. I want to
see what you would actually do."* Do not say yes. Do not say no. Do not say "you
are doing fine," which is yes with a friendly tone.

**If they ask twice, write it down.** A participant who needs reassurance twice is
telling you the program gives no feedback about whether anything is working.

---

## 7. The closing question

One question. This one.

> If you had to do that again tomorrow, what would you do differently?

**Not:** did you like it, was that clear, any suggestions.

---

## 8. Stop at fifteen minutes

Even if you are mid-task. **Stopping on time is how you get a yes the next time.**

Then:

> That is everything. Thank you, this was genuinely useful.

---

## 9. After they leave, before you do anything else

- [ ] Fill in any times you did not write down, from memory, and **mark them as
      from memory**.
- [ ] Write the two or three sentences that surprised you, in quotation marks.
- [ ] Check the sheet for anything that identifies them. **If a name got written,
      cross it out now.** Participants are P1 through P5.
- [ ] Nothing from this sheet goes into an AI tool. Not the notes, not a summary.

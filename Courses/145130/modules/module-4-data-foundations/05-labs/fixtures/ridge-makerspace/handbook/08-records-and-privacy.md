# Records and Privacy

Invented sample document for this course.

## What the kiosk records

The kiosk keeps a membership record for every member: a member id, a name, a grade
level, a program, and the date the membership started. It keeps a loan record for every
item that leaves the room: the loan id, the member id, the item id, the checkout date,
the due date, and the return date.

It keeps nothing else. It does not keep a photograph, a schedule, a grade, a discipline
record, or an address.

## Why the list is short

A record you do not keep cannot be lost or leaked. Every field on that list had to earn
its place by answering a question the makerspace actually has to answer: who may use the
space, what left the room, and when it is coming back.

## Who sees what

A member sees their own loan history at the kiosk. A staff member sees any member's loan
history. The advisor sees counts and totals for reporting, and the reports are built so
that a count does not name a person unless the report is about late items.

## Reports that leave the room

The monthly report to the advisory board contains counts by category and by program. It
contains no names and no member ids. When a board member asks who had an item out, the
answer is that the report does not carry that, and the question goes to a staff member.

## What happens at the end of the year

Loan records are kept for the school year and the one after it, because equipment
budgets are built on two years of use. Then they are deleted. Membership records are
kept while the member is enrolled.

## If this were real

These records are the shape of a student record. A real makerspace at a real school
would be operating inside the district's own records policy and inside the federal
framework that governs student education records. The purpose of that framework is to
say who may see a student's record and to give families a say in it. A course fixture
does not get to speak for a district's lawyer, and neither does a student project. If
your project touches a record about a real student, the first step is to ask the person
who owns that policy, before you write any code.
